§1Four axes

One space, four axes

Mode fluency is the practice of working with AI deliberately: knowing which stance you are in, what you must supply for it to hold, how much of the work you are watching, and how big an ask you are making. It is for people who already use AI every day and still get burned, and for the teams that have to show they did something about it.

The Stances, the Stack, the Specifying guide with the AI @ Workplace curriculum, and Felix Rieseberg's ambition types are not separate things. Together they map four axes of one space. Mode fluency is the skill of moving through it on purpose, and re-entering at the right point when the work goes wrong.

§2Mode confusion

Same words, different contract

In aviation, mode confusion is the pilot believing the automation is doing one thing while it does another. With AI the words of a prompt rarely tell you which stance you are in. Flip the switch: the prompt stays the same, the layer that carries the load moves.

§3The matrix

Each stance leans on one layer

A stance is the contract you think you have with the AI. A Stack layer is what you must actually supply for that contract to hold. Each stance leans on one layer more than the others; that is where its failure lives, and where the guardrail belongs.

Flip a stance on and its layer lights. Flip on every stance your team uses and the panel shows which layers your shared setup must make strong.

§4Altitude

AI @ Workplace, 101 to 601

How to use the calculator, for people who can already do the math by hand. Start where you are, not at the top. 101 to 301 are about chatbots: in a chatbot, you are between every turn. From 401 on, you set the goal and the guardrails, and then you are on the loop, not in it.

Where the cockpit analogy stops

Autopilots alarm when they disconnect; LLMs do not. Calculators are deterministic; LLMs are fluently wrong. Aviation has licences and accident reports; knowledge work has none, so the accountability has to be self-imposed. That is why the instruments on this page are habits and structures you supply, not features you buy.
