## 0. The one-paragraph answer

You asked whether there is a "meta" that encompasses "how to use the calculator" (AI @ Workplace, the Stack) and "what the
calculator enables and when to reach for it" (the Archetypes, ambition). There is, and you have already named it without
noticing: **mode fluency**. In the Stack document you define it narrowly as "recognizing which layer is failing and
intervening at the right level". Generalized, it is the whole discipline: *knowing which mode you are in, choosing it
deliberately, and re-entering at the right one when the work goes wrong*. Your four artifacts are not four things. They are
four axes of one space, and mode fluency is the skill of moving through it on purpose:

| Axis | The question | Your artifact | Modes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Stance** | What am I expecting from this exchange? | Archetypes v3 | Oracle, Genie, Tutor, Mirror, Adversary, Librarian, Interpreter, Simulator, Scribe, Executor, Muse, Miner, Therapist, Translator |
| **Structure** | What must I supply for that expectation to hold? | AI-Human Engineering Stack + Specifying Work | Prompt, Context, Intent, Judgment, Coherence; Evaluation, Harness |
| **Autonomy** | How much of the work do I watch? | Specifying Work (modes) + the 101→601 ladder | Assistive, Directed, Autonomous, Continuous |
| **Ambition** | How big is the ask? | Rieseberg's four types (adopted) | Speed, Altitude, Depth, Range |

The name is unclaimed (no prior use found in two independent searches, modefluency.com and .dev are free as of tonight),
it is yours by authorship, it is backed by a formal failure mode in aviation ("mode confusion"), and it uses a word every
office worker already understands from their phone and their car. The curriculum stays **AI @ Workplace** (the product a
buyer understands). The repo stays **agentic-engineering** for now (it rides Karpathy's generic term for SEO on the
developer slice). Mode Fluency is the method that makes both distinct, and the house brand is simply you.

## 1. How I got there (the navigation, briefly)

I used a concept-navigation pass rather than a survey. The moves that produced something:

**Climb the abstraction ladder.** Why operate well? To get the result with less effort. Why know your stance? To not be
betrayed by the tool's failure mode. Why the Stack? To diagnose at the right layer. The common genus: all four artifacts
describe *the human side of a delegation to a non-human intelligence*. Climb once more and you reach the question of who
decides, who holds the purpose, who is accountable. Your own corpus says it in four voices: the Stack ("the regress is
terminated by human governance"), the Archetypes ("delegation transfers labor, never responsibility"), Sivulka ("someone still
has to tell them what to do"), Nadella ("what you create should belong to you"). The meta-discipline is being the
accountable human who directs machine intelligence. In school terms: you may use the calculator, but you sign the exam.

**The thesis has a primary-source anchor.** Seymour Papert, *Mindstorms* (1980): "true computer literacy is not just knowing
how to make use of computers... It is knowing when it is appropriate to do so." That is your calculator intuition, stated
46 years ago about the previous instrument. The UK's Royal Society (2012) then retired the "ICT" brand because teaching had
collapsed into "how to use office software", and the 2013 curriculum led with "computational thinking" and put tool
literacy inside it. The lesson for you: name the thinking, and put the button-pressing inside it. (Citations: research/R4 [20][22][23].)

**Cross domains.** Who already solved "direct a capable, literal, partly unreliable agent and stay accountable"?
- *Principal-agent theory* (economics): the human is literally the principal, the software is literally the agent. Its three
  problems map onto your Stack: information asymmetry = context; objective divergence = intent; costly monitoring =
  judgment and evaluation. Negative transfer to flag: LLM agents are not self-interested; divergence comes from default
  priors, not incentives. "Principal" is generic and taken on every domain (research/R3), so it is a teaching frame, not a name.
- *Mission command / commander's intent* (military doctrine): give the why, the end state and the constraints, not the how.
  This is intent engineering's historical home and the spirit of your Specifying guide. Teaching import, not a brand.
- *Aviation automation*: the pilot-in-command rule, automation complacency, mode confusion, and a ratings ladder that looks
  exactly like 101→601 (student, private, instrument, commercial, airline transport). "Instrument-rated" means certified to
  fly when you cannot see outside, which is the Executor archetype exactly. Evidence in research/R4.
- *Calculators in mathematics education*: the curriculum moved from arithmetic to modeling; "when to reach for it" became
  the taught skill. Your analogy is historically sound. Evidence in research/R4.
- *Computer literacy → computational thinking* (Papert 1980, Wing 2006): the lesson for naming is that "literacy" names a
  tool skill and loses; a name for a thinking skill wins curricula. "AI literacy" is now a legal phrase (EU AI Act Art. 4),
  which makes it demand, not a brand.

**Turn the method on the venture.** Run your own Stack on this project and the binding constraint is not prompt or
context (you have abundant content). It is *intent* (what does the venture want: income, reputation, a career transition?)
and *coherence* (five artifacts, three names: agentic-engineering, AIES, PonicLabs). This brief is intent and coherence
engineering for the brand. That is also a credible hook for the site: "we ran our own audit protocol on ourselves".

**A new artifact fell out.** The Archetypes and the Stack describe the same delegation from two sides. An archetype is the
contract the human *thinks* they have. A Stack layer is what the human must actually *supply* for that contract to hold.
Crossing them gives a table nobody has published:

| Stance | Layer that carries the load | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Oracle | Judgment | The failure is unexamined trust; the fix is doubt. |
| Genie | Prompt | Wishes are literal; the fix is specification. |
| Interpreter | Evaluation | Confabulated meaning looks like insight; the fix is the evidence trail. |
| Executor | Harness + Judgment | The work happens unseen; the fix is structural checkpoints. |
| Miner | Context (writing) | "If you didn't record it, you never had it." The journal is written context. |
| Tutor | Intent | You must know what you want to learn before you can be taught. |
| Adversary | Intent | You must know what you are defending. |
| Scribe | Prompt | Craft follows format. |
| Simulator | Context | A rehearsal is only as good as the scenario you load. |
| Librarian | Evaluation | Retrieval must be checked against the source. |
| Mirror | Coherence | Recognition requires a stable reflection across sessions. |
| Translator | Context | Two incomplete maps must both be present. |
| Therapist | Judgment | The risk is dependency; the discipline is knowing when to stop. |
| Muse | (none) | The one stance that cannot be metric'd, which is why it stays hidden. |

This matrix is the natural centerpiece of the website and the spine of the assessment product.

**Follow the surprise.** The most surprising single finding was that the name was already in your document. The second was
that the gap analysis (research/R1) independently lists "no named, teachable method for diagnosing a failure" and "no one
teaches how to operate AI and which stance to take as one discipline" as the two clearest open positions in the market.
Mode fluency is the name of exactly those two gaps.

### 3.6 Two corrections to make in your existing documents (from research/R4)
- **The METR "19% slower" figure is dated.** Both the Stack document and the Specifying guide open with it. METR's own
  February 2026 update calls its newer data "an unreliable signal": point estimates moved toward a speedup, both intervals
  include zero, and developers increasingly refused to work without AI. Keep the July 2025 result as history; do not present
  it as the current state. (R4 [25][26].)
- **Training alone does not fix the Executor problem.** Parasuraman & Manzey (2010) find automation bias "cannot be prevented
  by training or instructions" and affects experts. This is an argument *for* your product mix: an assessment that installs
  structural checks (harness, checkpoints, evaluation) is worth more than a workshop alone, and the site should say so. (R4 [11].)
- Also from R4, the disanalogies to state up front whenever you use the calculator or cockpit analogies: calculators are
  deterministic and LLMs are fluently wrong; autopilots alarm when they disconnect and LLMs do not; calculators automate a
  skill *below* the problem while LLMs reach into framing and reasoning itself; aviation has licences and accident reports and
  knowledge work has none, so the accountability must be self-imposed.

- The name sits next to Anthropic's "AI Fluency". That is deliberate adjacency, but if Anthropic ever ships "modes" as a
  product term the name gets crowded. Mitigation: the essay and the matrix are the moat, not the two words.
- No trademark search was possible tonight. Do one before spending on the name.
- The co-author pseudonym: a journalist or a client who searches the pen name will find your old YouTube handle. Decide a line
  now ("a pen name I used for drafting with AI") and stick to it.
- Selling to SMEs is outreach work you have not done yet. The 90-day plan assumes warm-network conversations first.
- Live delivery across time zones conflicts with the day job. Price for scarcity rather than volume.
