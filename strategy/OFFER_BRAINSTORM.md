# Free, paid, and what to build next — a brainstorm (2026-10-22)

**Your rule:** give the resources away; charge for what needs your human time and judgment. That rule is not just ethical, it is
the correct economics for a solo side project: free assets compound reach; paid time compounds trust. The question is only
*which* things sit on which side, and what new things are worth making.

## The line
| Free (compounds reach) | Paid (needs you) | Later, productized (needs software or an audience) |
|---|---|---|
| AI @ Workplace videos and slides 101→601 | **Mode Fluency Assessment** (fixed fee, 2 weeks) | Self-serve course from workshop recordings |
| The essay, the Stances, the Stack, the Matrix, the Specifying guide | **AI @ Workplace workshops** on the team's own workflows | **Team edition of the Diagnostic** (see below) |
| **The Mode Fluency Diagnostic skill** and prompt pack | **Workflow Delegation Sprint**: one recurring workflow, harnessed and evaluated, team taught | Evals starter kits per job family |
| The site's self-test, the matrix explorer | Fractional "head of AI practice" retainer for an SME | Train-the-trainer for L&D departments |
| Harness starter kits (folder layout, instruction file, checks) as templates | Talks and in-house keynotes | A lightweight "rated" credential for teams who complete 101→401 and the assessment |

## Why the Diagnostic should be free, and why it still sells
The free skill is the top of the funnel *and* the proof of method. Someone who runs it gets a real diagnosis; a team lead who
runs it discovers that the finding they cannot act on alone is exactly what the assessment is for. Give away the diagnosis;
charge for the prescription, the implementation, and the accountability. This is how the best doctors, lawyers and auditors
have always worked, and it survives model releases because the scarce part is you.

## What is genuinely new to build (ranked by value ÷ effort for you)
1. **The Diagnostic skill** (being built now). Free. Occupies the open position nobody holds: a non-technical team's practice
   shipped as files that people and their agents can both use.
2. **Team edition of the Diagnostic** (software, small). Each member runs the interview privately; results aggregate into a
   team stance profile, a shared binding layer, and an evidence pack formatted for the EU AI Act Article 4 record. Priced per
   team (€/$ 290–990). It is the same logic as the skill plus a form and a report generator; it can start as a spreadsheet
   template and become a web tool when demand shows. This is the first thing you could sell that does not consume your hours.
3. **Harness starter kits by job family** (free). Ops, finance, HR, sales, legal: a folder, an instruction file, the three
   checks that matter, one example workflow. Each kit is a 401 lesson made concrete and a lead magnet for the Sprint.
4. **Evals starter** (paid service, later a kit). "Evals are the new OKRs." Help a team write its first ten checks for a
   workflow and wire them into the harness. High value, teaches the 301/401 material by doing.
5. **Self-serve course** (paid, months 9–12). Only after workshops have produced recordings, exercises and testimonials, and
   only when the audience is big enough that 1–2% conversion pays for the build.

## Things I would not do
- Sell certificates against Google and Coursera. A team "rating" tied to real artifacts (their harness, their checks) is
  different and can come later.
- Charge for skill files. The market gives them away; the money is in services built on them and in hosted tools.
- Build a general "AI consultancy" page. The narrow promise (know which mode you're in, fix the binding layer) is the moat.

## The pricing ladder as the customer sees it
free videos → free diagnostic → $49–149 course (later) → $1,200+ workshop → $3,500+ assessment → $8–15k sprint → retainer.
Every rung produces the material for the next and none requires the previous to be bought.
