Prepared 2026-10-21 for the orchestrator and Henrique. Scope: who owns which words and frames, where the open ground is, and who could crush a solo creator. Per the brief, this proposes no brand names, does not evaluate Henrique's frameworks and contains no marketing copy.

**Pattern.** Each "X engineering" term goes from being coined, to an influential endorsement, to a third-party "complete guide", to generic, in roughly 3 to 8 months. Context engineering went from June 2025 to repo descriptions. Harness engineering went from Feb 2026 to third-party guides. Owning a single "X engineering" layer term is not defensible. What stays owned is a named *whole* (4D, Co-Intelligence, Frontier Firm, AI Proficiency Report), not a layer name.

## 3. Who else frames human-AI interaction as archetypes, stances or roles

1. **Centaur vs cyborg** (Mollick, 2023-09-16). Two work styles: a clean division of labor vs fluid interleaving. https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/centaurs-and-cyborgs-on-the-jagged
2. **"Treat AI like a person (but tell it what kind of person it is)"** (Mollick's rule 3, *Co-Intelligence*, 2024). Here the role is assigned to the AI, not chosen as a stance by the human. https://erudit.org/fr/revues/cjils/2024-v47-n1-cjils09364/1111737ar.pdf
3. **Agent boss / human-agent teams** (Microsoft, 2025-04-23). The human becomes a manager of agents. https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2025/04/23/the-2025-annual-work-trend-index-the-frontier-firm-is-born/
4. **Automation / augmentation / agency** (Anthropic AI Fluency). Three modes ordered by how much AI autonomy there is. https://www.theneuron.ai/explainer-articles/inside-anthropics-ai-fluency-masterclass-on-human-ai-collaboration
5. **17 human-LLM archetypes** (Chappidi et al., CHI 2026, submitted 2026-02-12). Role patterns in decision support, tested on clinical cases. It shows that the choice of archetype changes the LLM's outputs. https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11924
6. **Six-mode autonomy taxonomy**, from Human-Out-of-the-Loop to Human-Augmented Model (arXiv 2507.14034, July 2025). Covers technical support. https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.14034
7. **"AI as Teammate or Tool?"** (review of decision-support literature, arXiv 2602.15865, 2026). https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15865
8. **Taxonomy of developer-AI interaction** (arXiv 2501.08774, Jan 2025). https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.08774
9. **"Work with AI, not use AI": teammate, not tool** (Jeremy Utley, Oct 2025). https://elearning.adobe.com/2025/10/adobe-learning-summit-2025-highlights/
10. **Delegate / teammate / autonomous agent** (Allie K. Miller, 2026-02-12). https://www.wdbj7.com/video/2026/02/12/how-become-an-ai-super-user-generation-ai-new-series/
11. **User mental models drifting from "software" to "human-like"** (analysis of more than 200k conversations, *J. Intelligent Information Systems*, 2025). https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10844-025-00960-6

**What these have in common.** Almost every frame sits on one of two axes:
- **Autonomy and control.** How much the AI does vs the human (items 3, 4, 6, 10).
- **Social role.** Tool vs teammate vs person (items 2, 7, 9).

Frames that classify by *what the human is trying to get from the exchange* (decode material, rehearse a situation, provoke ideas, reflect their own thinking, mine for variations) are rare. The one rigorous archetype taxonomy (item 5) is academic and limited to decision-making. Mollick's centaur/cyborg is a binary about work style, not a palette of stances. This is observed from the sources listed. Absence of evidence is not proof, so treat it as a strong signal, not a certainty.

## 4. Gap analysis

1. **No one teaches "how to operate AI" and "which stance to take, and when" as one discipline.** The operating side is courses and guides (rows 7, 8, 9, 12, 13, 14, 15, 22, 23). The stance side is essays and keynotes (rows 3, 6, 19, 21). Anthropic's 4D (row 1) comes closest: it pairs Delegation with Description and Discernment. But its stance model is three coarse autonomy modes. *Why it is open:* vendors teach usage of their own product, thought leaders publish essays rather than curricula, and the academic taxonomies stay inside their papers.

2. **Engineering-grade concepts have not been translated for non-technical knowledge workers.** All the depth vocabulary (context, harness, agentic and loop engineering; 12-factor agents; Willison's patterns) targets developers (rows 5, 12, 13, 14, 15, 23). The non-technical offers stop at fundamentals and prompting: Google's course runs "under 5 hours" (row 7), AI For Everyone (row 8), and Section's survey-led proficiency training (row 9). *Why it is open:* developers are the loud, paying early audience, and translating the material without dumbing it down needs someone who knows both worlds.

3. **No named, teachable method for diagnosing a failure** ("which part broke, and where do I re-enter?"). Incumbents teach checklists and competencies. Section reports proficiency stuck at about 10% even as spending rose (row 9). BCG finds only about a third of employees properly trained (row 16). Commentary on Anthropic's modes points to "mode mismatch" as the main reason output is weak, yet nobody owns a diagnostic practice for it, and "mode fluency" is unclaimed (section 2). *Why it is open:* diagnosis is harder to package than a framework acronym, and it needs failure-mode content, which vendors have little incentive to publish about their own products.

4. **No practitioner-grade stance taxonomy for everyday knowledge work organized by the human's intent.** Existing taxonomies are domain-bound: decision support (rows 5 and 7 in section 3), software engineering (item 8), technical support (item 6). Practitioner frames are binaries or autonomy ladders (section 3). *Why it is open:* academics publish within one domain, and creators prefer simple two-way contrasts that spread easily.

5. **Curriculum shipped as installable, agent-readable artifacts for non-technical teams.** The open-source curricula with traction are developer repos: 79.4k, 26.8k and 9.3k stars (rows 22, 23). Commercial offers for knowledge workers are video, cohorts or seats (rows 7, 9, 10, 18). No one in the table ships a non-technical team's practice as files that people *and* their agents can both use. Anthropic's Claude Academy (row 2) could move here, but only for its own product. *Why it is open:* course businesses monetize seats and hours, and repo authors serve developers.

6. **Implementation-level help for SMEs in their own workflows, vendor-neutral, priced between free courses and enterprise consulting.** Price points in the table: free (rows 1, 8, 12), $41–82/month (row 9), $49/month (row 7), $750/seat for teams under 100 (row 9), $4,200 per cohort (row 10), and undisclosed enterprise programs (rows 16, 17). Section covers SME *seat training*. The narrower gap is hands-on rebuilding of a small team's own workflows. Microsoft introduces a "human-agent ratio" but says it is task-specific and gives no method for setting it (row 6). *Why it is open:* consultancies need enterprise deal sizes, and course companies avoid custom services.

7. **Version-proof fundamentals with a visible update discipline.** Ruben Hassid reportedly says "I make a course today, in two months it's completely irrelevant" ([secondary](https://wanderwealth.substack.com/p/how-to-use-ai-a-chat-with-the-internets), UNVERIFIED quote). The "X engineering" terms turn over within months (section 2). No incumbent markets "principles that survive model releases, with versioned updates" as its core promise. *Why it is open:* creators are rewarded for being first on the news, and enterprises buy certificates.

## 5. Threats, and how to be complementary rather than competitive

**Could crush a solo creator:**
- **Anthropic (rows 1, 2, 15).** A free, credentialed course with academic co-authors, Coursera distribution and the "4D" vocabulary. A secondary source claims more than 1M enrollments (UNVERIFIED). *Complement:* treat 4D as the shared baseline, map onto it explicitly ("if you know the 4Ds, this is what comes next"), and stay vendor-neutral where Anthropic cannot be.
- **Microsoft (row 6).** "Agent boss" and "Frontier Firm" reach every M365 buyer by default. *Complement:* turn its leader-level concepts into practice for small teams. Its own post admits the human-agent ratio is task-specific and offers no method for setting it.
- **Section (row 9).** The closest direct competitor for the primary customer (non-technical knowledge workers and SME teams). It has a branded annual research series, small-team pricing and an AI coach. *Complement:* do not compete on seat training or survey research. Compete on depth, diagnosis and implementation, which Section's catalog format does not do. Section's statistics are also free, citable evidence of the problem.
- **Mollick (row 3).** Owns the stance and "how to think about AI" discourse among professionals and educators. *Complement:* cite and build on his work (the jagged frontier is a reason stances need choosing). Do not compete for "co-intelligence".
- **Nate B. Jones (row 4).** Publishes very often. Owns "intent engineering" in the creator market and already teaches a multi-skill progression (prompt to context to intent). That is the closest public analogue to a layered model. *Complement:* acknowledge the shared term and differentiate on audience (non-technical teams) and on diagnosis. Expect people to assume the work is derivative if the overlap is not acknowledged.
- **Karpathy, Willison, Osmani (rows 5, 12, 13).** Own developer-side terminology and credibility. "Agentic engineering" is now Karpathy's generic term, and Henrique's repo uses that name (71 stars vs 79.4k for the top curriculum repo). That is a discoverability and differentiation risk on the developer slice. *Complement:* link to them as the developer reference shelf and position as the bridge to people who are not developers.
- **Academic archetype work (section 3, item 5).** A CHI 2026 paper already claims "human-LLM archetypes". *Complement:* cite it, and differentiate by domain (everyday knowledge work, not clinical decision support) and by purpose (teachable stances, not design patterns).
- **Google, DeepLearning.AI, Coursera (rows 7, 8).** Cheap certificates with brand credentials. *Complement:* do not sell certificates. Position as what you do *after* you have one.
- **BCG and Accenture (rows 16, 17).** Not a direct threat, because they serve enterprises. Their research (for example BCG: only about a third properly trained, more use after 5 or more hours of training) shows the need exists.

**Anomalies and open questions for the orchestrator**

- The O'Reilly listing for Osmani's *Agentic Engineering* shows a 2027 publication date, which may be an error (secondary, UNVERIFIED).
- Prices for Tiago Forte's AI Second Brain, Ruben Hassid's paid tiers and Nate B. Jones's paid tier could not be verified.
- The negative findings (no prior owner of "mode fluency"; few intent-based stance taxonomies) each rest on 1 or 2 searches. A trademark or USPTO check and a deeper literature search would make them firmer.
