# R12. Scalable products: a navigation pass (2026-11-01)

Written by the orchestrating agent with the noonautics method (five axes of movement, follow the surprise, validate against breakthrough
signatures). Grounding with benchmarks follows in R14. R11 (2026-10-29) is the prior ranking; this note looks for what R11 could not see.

## The stuckness, in one sentence
Every product that costs nothing per sale needs an audience Henrique does not have, and everything that builds the audience costs the
hours he does not have; R11 confirmed that none of twelve candidates replaces one assessment in year one at current reach.

## Axis 1: abstraction (what is actually being sold?)
Climb from "a course", "a kit", "a template" to the type: Mode Fluency sells **encoded judgment about how to work with AI**. Climb once more:
the thing institutions pay for at that level is not content but **a standard and the proof of meeting it**. ISO sells PDFs nobody reads for
pleasure; the asset is the authority to say "this conforms". Descend on a different branch: the scalable products are not lessons but
**artefacts that carry the standard**: a spec format others write to, a check list others run, a record others can file.
Surprise: the content (videos, documents) is the least valuable thing to sell; the vocabulary and the proof are the asset.

## Axis 2: cross-domain (who solved "solo expert, free body of knowledge, scalable income"?)
- **Scrum.org and the Linux Foundation:** the Guide is free; the exam is paid and automated; employers demand the certificate. Structure to
  preserve: a free canonical text + a paid, automated proof of understanding that a third party values. Earlier decisions refused
  "certification" because nobody demands an AI-fluency certificate. See axis 4 for why that changed.
- **Standards bodies and style guides (ISO, the Chicago Manual, Keep a Changelog, Conventional Commits):** publish the format, let adoption
  do the marketing, sell the reference and the conformance tools. Structure: a named, versioned spec with a badge.
- **Aviation checklists:** the product is the checklist, not the training. Structure: a per-workflow evaluation pack.
- **Driving instruction:** lessons are cheap and local; the test is the scalable product. Same shape as Scrum.org.
Surface look-alikes to avoid: "a Udemy course" (shares surface with Scrum.org's training but not the demand structure); "a newsletter" (audience
first, product never).

## Axis 3: self-reference (apply the method to its own rule)
Mode Fluency's founding rule is "give the resources away, charge for human time". The stuckness is that rule. The distinction "free resources
vs paid time" has a blind spot: **paid artefacts that are not time**. Two moves follow. (1) Apply the diagnostic to artefacts instead of people:
the Redline tier (a human reads your instruction file) has a software form, a linter that scores an instruction file or spec against the
Stack's layers and the lexical checks (vague quantifiers, softened directives, negations, missing success criteria). Client-side, no server,
no data held. (2) Apply the Stack's Evaluation layer to the practice itself: the site's own QA gate is a template for "ten checks per workflow"
sold as packs.

## Axis 4: time (what constraint changed?)
Dormancy detection: "AI literacy certificates" were a pointless idea in 2023 because nobody needed proof. Since Article 4 of the EU AI Act
applies (national enforcement from August 2026; the July 2026 amendment keeps "take measures to support" literacy binding on SMEs, per R2),
an SME needs **a written record of the measures it took**. A scored, automated, per-person assessment with a dated record is exactly such a
measure. The constraint that blocked the idea has changed; the earlier refusal should be revisited on that ground alone. Forward: within a year
agents run SME workflows and the scarce human act is the check and the sign-off; per-workflow evaluation packs will be the thing people
search for. Ghost path: prompt libraries died; harness kits are their living form.

## Axis 5: resolution (what grain is solvable?)
R11 priced at the grain of single products ($49 kit, $99 course). Zoom out one step: a **single library membership** (all kits, templates,
checks, records; one price; updated monthly) is one SKU to market instead of twelve, and it turns every new artefact into a reason to renew
rather than a new launch. Zoom in further ($5 checklists) and the signal drowns in marketing noise. The solvable grain is the library, with the
record as its entry ticket.

## Where the surprise is
1. **The record, not the course.** An "AI literacy record": a 30–40 minute online assessment built from the fourteen stances and the Stack's
   failure signatures, scored automatically, producing a dated per-person record a company can file under Article 4. Priced per person. The
   assessment the human delivers for $3,500 becomes the evidence pack's wholesale; the record is its retail. Must never be called a certification
   of competence: it is a record of a literacy measure taken, which is the thing the regulation names.
2. **The linter, not the Redline.** A free client-side "spec lint" (skill, CLI and a browser page) that scores any instruction file against the
   Stack and the lexical checks; the paid layer is the team report or the library. Zero marginal cost, builds the vocabulary, feeds the teardown.
3. **The standard, not the content.** Publish the Intent Doc / Minimal Viable Spec format as a named, versioned spec ("written to the Mode
   Fluency spec" badge). No direct revenue; it is the marketing engine for 1 and 2 and the thing the linter checks.
4. **The library, not the storefront.** One membership, everything inside, monthly additions.
5. **The book.** The Stack, the Stances and the essay already are one; a book is the oldest zero-marginal-cost product and the one that
   buys authority with strangers. Slow, small money, large credibility.

## Validation against the signatures
Cross-domain transfer (Scrum.org structure into Article 4 evidence) and a counterfactual on a foundational assumption (free resources vs
paid time) both fired. Compression: "sell the proof, not the lesson." The move that was previously impossible: a product whose demand does not
depend on Henrique's audience, because a regulation supplies the demand.

## Non-navigable parts, flagged honestly
Reach is still a resource problem, not a thinking problem: even the record needs a first ten companies, which come from the assessment and the
warm network. And the record carries legal and reputational risk if it is oversold; the grounding pass must check what Article 4 evidence
actually needs to be and what competitors already sell as "AI literacy training with certificate" in the EU.
