# N2: Concept-anchored umbrella names (AI/agent word + the core idea)

Run date: 2026-10-22. Agent: N2 (concept-anchored). Generated from knowledge only: no web research, no collision checks.
Every "risk" below is foreseen, not verified; a separate agent checks collisions. Scores are N2's own, 1–5, and are not
ranked against N1/N3.

**Method.** I started from the ideas in STRATEGY_BRIEF §0–§1 and the Matrix, not from words: (a) know which mode you are
in; (b) the stance you take before you ask; (c) the human as principal/pilot-in-command who directs; (d) delegation transfers
labor, never responsibility ("you sign the exam"); (e) the calculator problem / knowing *when* (Papert); (f) the ratings
ladder 101→601. For each idea I looked for the shortest form that pairs it with AI / Agent / Machine / Intelligence /
Automation. Names already in research/R3 as TAKEN, CROWDED or GENERIC were excluded (e.g. AI Direction, Human in Command,
Agent Boss, Agent Fluency, Pilot in Command, Directing/Conducting Intelligence). Adjacent ones are flagged in the risk column.

**Two collisions to know about before reading the table** (from general knowledge, UNVERIFIED in this pass):
Google Search's product is called **"AI Mode"** (2025), which shadows every "AI + Mode" name; and **Modal** is a well-known
serverless compute company, which shadows "Modal AI"-style names for a CTO audience.

## 2. Top 8 (N2's picks, not ranked against other agents)

1. **Agentwise** — One word, nine characters, with the agent word up front and the human quality ("wise") as the suffix, so it says
   competence, not product. It stretches over everything: Agentwise 101–601, the Agentwise Matrix, an Agentwise audit, Agentwise skills.
   *Tagline: "Know which mode you're in."*
2. **Deliberate AI** — It names the behavior the whole method trains: choosing the stance, the layer and the autonomy level on
   purpose instead of by reflex. A COO can write "Deliberate AI program" on a procurement form without explaining anything.
   *Tagline: "Every AI move, on purpose."*
3. **Agent Stance** — It brings the Archetypes into the brand: the expectation you set before you hand an agent anything,
   then the layer you check. It is the most concept-specific name here and pairs naturally with the Matrix ("the Agent Stance Matrix").
   *Tagline: "Decide what you expect before the machine answers."*
4. **Delegated Intelligence** — It holds the core sentence of the corpus, that delegation transfers labor and never
   responsibility, and it sounds like a field rather than a gadget. Long on a slide, but very enterprise-credible.
   *Tagline: "Intelligence delegated. Responsibility kept."*
5. **AI-Rated** — It turns the 101→601 ladder into a credential people can claim ("our analysts are AI-Rated"), borrowing
   aviation's ratings without the jargon. It is the name that sells the curriculum and assessment hardest.
   *Tagline: "Rated to fly the machine you were handed."*
6. **Agent Mandate** — In principal-agent terms, a mandate is exactly what the human gives: purpose, scope, limits, sign-off.
   It suits the consulting and specification side especially well ("mandate design workshop").
   *Tagline: "Give agents a mandate, not a wish."*
7. **Human-Led AI** — The most unmistakable statement that the human is in charge, which is exactly what the
   enterprise buyer is anxious about. Its weakness is ownability, so it may work better as the positioning line under another name.
   *Tagline: "The machine does the labor. You keep the call."*
8. **Modewise AI** — It keeps "mode", the idea Henrique already authored, while dropping the long and incumbent-adjacent "Fluency"
   and adding the AI word he asked for. The Google "AI Mode" shadow is weaker here because "Modewise" is the head word.
   *Tagline: "Choose the mode. Then write the prompt."*
