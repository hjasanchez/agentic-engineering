**Your constraints:** a direct AI/agent/automation reference; short; enterprise-credible; mass reach; the human stays in charge.
**Process:** three generators (enterprise register, concept-anchored, mass-market) produced 120 candidates with no web access;
I shortlisted 32; a clearance agent checked domains (RDAP), exact-name companies, USPTO marks, GitHub and search shadowing.
EU and WIPO trademark databases blocked automated access, so a European mark check is still open. Files: N1–N4 in this folder.

Two structural findings: (1) Google's search product "AI Mode" shadows every name containing "mode", which also argues
against "Mode Fluency" on discoverability; (2) the names that two generators produced independently (Agentwise, Human-Led AI,
Agent Lead, AI Gears, Steer AI) are the ones the market has also converged on, which is why most are taken or generic.

## My recommendation: **Agent Stance**
- Says AI: "Agent" is now mainstream vocabulary (Microsoft's "agent boss", "AI agents" in every keynote) and it is the word
  that still fits at AI 601 (local agents, dark factories), where "chatbot" or "LLM" would not.
- Says the concept: a stance is the human's position toward the machine. The Fourteen Stances become the flagship product,
  the stance × layer matrix becomes "the Agent Stance matrix", and "know which mode you're in" survives as the tagline.
- Says who is in charge: the stance belongs to the person, never to the agent.
- Enterprise-credible: "the Agent Stance assessment", "Agent Stance workshops", "an Agent Stance for your team" all read
  naturally in a procurement form. Eleven characters, two syllables each, no spelling ambiguity.
- Clean: agentstance.com and agentstance.ai are available (RDAP, 2026-10-22); no exact-name company; no USPTO hit; no
  GitHub org; search results are academic only (Dennett's "intentional stance", LLM-stance papers), which is a good
  neighbourhood. Register both domains before deciding anything else.
- Risk: "stance" is a little abstract for mass search, so the YouTube series keeps the plain title "AI @ Workplace" as the
  funnel into the brand. European trademark check still to do.

## Runner-up: **AI Gears** (the mass-market route)
"Know which gear you're in" is the mode idea in words every driver understands, and automatic vs manual transmission is a
perfect everyday picture of the autonomy ladder (assistive → continuous). Clean on incumbents; aigears.ai available, .com
registered by an unidentified owner. Downside: playful rather than authoritative; a COO might hesitate to put it on a
training-budget line. Works brilliantly as the name of the curriculum or the diagnostic even if the umbrella is Agent Stance.

## Also viable
- **Modewise AI**: keeps your "mode" continuity and adds "-wise" (judgment). Clean, both domains available. But Google's
  AI Mode shadows it in search and the "-wise AI" shape reads like a product that does the work for you.
- **Delegated Intelligence**: the most enterprise-sounding and concept-rich (delegate the work, keep the responsibility),
  but long (22 characters) and crowded as a phrase.
- **Agent Mandate**: excellent for the consulting line ("give agents a mandate, not a wish") and the skills pack; too
  narrow and too corporate for a mass channel.

## If you loved one of the taken ones
Agentwise's incumbent is a side project, but all three domains are registered and a GitHub org exists; you would be
building on someone else's name. Agents at Work is a real firm with staff. I would not fight either.
