**Evidence status.** No web research was done in this pass, as the brief requires. Every collision named in the "risk" column
comes from general knowledge and is **UNVERIFIED** until the collision-check agent runs it. "No known collision" means only
that I know of none. No domain or trademark claim is made anywhere in this file.

**Excluded on purpose** (incumbent-owned or already ruled out in R3): AI Fluency and every "-fluency" variant, Agent Boss,
Frontier Firm, Co-Intelligence, Intent Engineering, Agentic Engineering, Operator, Copilot, AgentOps, AIES, Directing
Intelligence (and the near-identical "Directed Intelligence"), Articulate, Conductor, Cockpit, Agentcraft, Tokencraft,
Workcraft, Specwork, all Principal variants (incl. the tempting "Agent Principal"), Mission Command, Commander's Intent,
Agentic Literacy, plus descriptors like AI Training / AI Consulting / AI Academy / AI Literacy.

**Pattern used.** Every name has an AI-class anchor (AI, Agent/Agentic, LLM, Automation, Model, Machine, Intelligence) plus an
enterprise-register element: a Latinate root (Modus, Agentry, Agentive), a practice noun (Practice, Method, Standard, Works,
Grade, Kata), or a word for authority that stays human (Mandate, Directed, Command, Steering, Conduct). Test applied to each:
"Is this a phrase a COO would type into a procurement form?" and "Does '\_\_\_-certified team' work?"

## 2. Top 8 (pitch and tagline)

**1. Modus AI** (score 4)
*Modus* is Latin for mode, measure and method, so the name carries the whole thesis (know which mode you're in, work by method) in
a word that already sounds like a firm. It brands cleanly in tiers: "AI @ Workplace, by Modus AI", "the Modus diagnostic",
"a Modus-certified team".
Tagline: **"The method for working with AI."** (alt: "Know your mode.")

**2. Agentry** (score 4)
A real dictionary word for "the work of an agent". It sits in the same family as *ministry* and *registry*, so it reads as an
institution, while it stays short enough for a LinkedIn headline. It names a whole field (the stance, the stack, the autonomy
levels) without sounding like a product that does the work, and "Agentry-certified" sounds like a credential.
Tagline: **"The craft of directing AI agents."**

**3. Directed AI** (score 4)
It uses Henrique's own autonomy vocabulary and makes the core promise in two words: the human directs and the AI is directed.
The idea is clear to an office worker at once and precise for a CTO. Check carefully that it does not drift toward the 2027
*Directing Intelligence* book.
Tagline: **"You direct. It delivers."**

**4. AI Kata** (score 4)
Operations and HR leaders already know kata as rehearsed standard work (Toyota Kata), so the name makes AI capability a
trained habit rather than a tool rollout. It fits a curriculum of drills (101→601), workshops ("kata sessions") and later
skills ("installable katas").
Tagline: **"Practice that turns AI into judgment."**

**5. Agent Mandate** (score 3)
In law, banking and consulting, a mandate is the scoped authority a principal gives an agent. That is exactly what Specifying
Work and the autonomy modes teach. It is the most enterprise-native name on the list, but the "AI mandate" (a CEO memo telling
staff to use AI) reading must be checked.
Tagline: **"Give AI a mandate, not a free hand."**

**6. AI Mode Standard** (score 3)
This keeps the mode thesis Henrique liked and adds the explicit "AI" and a certification register ("AI Mode Standard, Level
3"). It works best if the brand later becomes a published open standard (the GitHub repo is the natural home), and it is
weaker as a firm name for a solo founder.
Tagline: **"The standard for knowing which mode you're in."**

**7. The Agent Practice** (score 3)
It uses the professional-services register exactly: assessments, workshops and retained advice from "a practice", which a
COO buys without explanation. It is generic on its own, so it works best as the consulting arm under a distinctive house
name (for example "The Agentry Practice" or "Modus AI Practice").
Tagline: **"Where teams learn to run AI, not be run by it."**

**8. AI Machinist** (score 3)
A machinist is the skilled human who sets up, runs and measures the machine. That is the calculator analogy turned into a
role people can aspire to and be certified in ("Certified AI Machinist"). It is strong for individual learners and technical
teams, and needs testing with HR buyers for blue-collar connotations.
Tagline: **"The machine does the cutting. You set the tolerances."**

## 3. Five wildcards (each breaks one rule on purpose)

| Wildcard | Rule broken | Why it might still be worth it |
|---|---|---|
| **Stance & Stack** | #1 (no AI word) | It names the two halves of the method (the 14 stances × the AI-Human Engineering Stack) with alliteration and the shape of a law firm ("Stance & Stack LLP"). The AI reference would have to come from the tagline. |
| **The Human-Directed AI Standard** | #2 (four words, long) | It is the most explicit statement of constraint 5 and reads as a published standard. It could be the formal name of the open spec, with a shorter umbrella above it. |
| **AI Modeset** | #3 (a pun: mode + mindset) | It is readable at once by most people, keeps the Mode thesis Henrique liked, adds "AI" and is shorter than Mode Fluency. Some buyers will find it cute. |
| **AI Standing Orders** | #4 (too narrow for the umbrella) | Naval "standing orders" are instructions that persist until revoked, which is exactly what skills, system prompts and agent policies are. It is an excellent name for the later installable-skills or software line. |
| **Mode Engine** | #5 (sounds like software that does the work) | It is a ready-made name for the paid software tier (a diagnostic that tells you which stance, layer and autonomy level you are in). It suits the product but not the umbrella. |

## 4. Notes for the orchestrator and the collision-check agent
- Priority checks for the top 8: "Modus AI" and "ModusAI", "Agentry" (SAP legacy product status, any AI startup), "Directed AI"
  (any brand, and distance from the Jansen book), "AI Kata" (any course or brand), "Agent Mandate", "AI Machinist".
- Compositions worth testing as an architecture: house = **Agentry** or **Modus AI**. Method = "the mode thesis" (stances ×
  stack × autonomy × ambition). Curriculum = AI @ Workplace 101→601. Consulting = "the Agentry Practice". Credential =
  "Agentry Grade 1-6" or "Modus-certified". Skills/software = "Standing Orders" / "Mode Engine".
- "LLM" and "Bot" anchors test poorly for this audience: LLM is opaque to office workers and Bot sounds cheap to enterprises.
  "AI" and "Agent" are the only anchors that pass both.
