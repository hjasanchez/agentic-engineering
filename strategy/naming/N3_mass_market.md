# N3: Umbrella-name candidates optimised for mass reach

Run date: 2026-10-22. Agent: N3 (mass market, creator-economy angle). No web research in this pass. Every collision note below
comes from memory and is **UNVERIFIED** until the separate collision agent checks it. Scores are mine, 1–5, judged on the
N3 brief: people search for it, remember it after one hearing, and it works as a YouTube channel, a LinkedIn page, an @handle
and a course title at the same time, while meeting the five hard rules in N_naming_common.md.

## How to read this (3 lines)
- Mass reach and ownability pull against each other. The names people already search for ("AI at work", "work with AI") are
  the hardest to own and rank for. I say how generic each one is instead of pretending otherwise.
- The best mass names below take a plain search phrase and add one ownable twist, such as a job title (Agent Lead), a metaphor
  (AI Gears) or a stance word (-wise).
- "Agent" has a second everyday meaning (call-centre agent, estate agent, insurance agent). That helps with plain-English
  familiarity and hurts SEO. Every Agent-* name carries this risk.

## 2. Top 8 (in no particular order across agents; ordered here by my score, then by mass-reach fit)

1. **Agents at Work**: It extends "AI @ Workplace" into a phrase people already say and type, with the rhythm of a show title,
   and it holds the warning that agents are working and someone must be in charge. It is also a natural YouTube channel, a
   LinkedIn newsletter and a workshop name all at once.
   *Tagline: "Agents at work. You in charge."*
2. **AgentWise**: One short word, an easy @handle, and the "-wise" suffix sells judgment, which is exactly what free courses
   do not teach. It leaves room for the method ("the AgentWise Assessment"), the stances and later software.
   *Tagline: "Know when to trust the machine."*
3. **Agent Lead**: It gives every office worker a role to claim and a title to put on LinkedIn, which turns the audience into
   the distribution channel. It also scales to a certificate and a team offer ("train your Agent Leads").
   *Tagline: "Every team needs one. Be the one."*
4. **AI Gears**: This is the plain-language version of Henrique's core idea (choose the right mode, re-enter where it failed),
   in a metaphor everyone has felt in their hands: the driver picks the gear. It avoids "mode", which Google's AI Mode now
   owns in search, and it gives a visual language for the site and the animations (gears, shifting, a gearbox for the Stack).
   *Tagline: "Know which gear you're in."*
5. **Human-Led AI**: It states the thesis in words a COO can paste into a procurement form or a board slide unchanged, and it
   lines up with the EU AI Act framing of "literacy measures". It is moderately generic, so it is best treated as a category
   claim that Henrique defines first, with a sharper sub-brand underneath.
   *Tagline: "AI does the work. People lead it."*
6. **Run Your AI**: An imperative that works as a channel name, a hook and a call to action, and it is the easiest name here to
   turn into a short video ("3 ways to run your AI instead of it running you"). It stretches to 601 (running local agents).
   *Tagline: "Run your AI, or it runs you."*
7. **AI Habits**: It borrows the most proven mass-market self-improvement register and turns the fourteen stances into
   habits people can adopt one at a time. It is the friendliest name here for non-technical learners, and still reads fine
   on an invoice.
   *Tagline: "Small habits. Serious AI."*
8. **AI @ Workplace (promoted to umbrella)**: Zero switching cost. The talks, slides and repo already carry it, and buyers
   understand it at once. Honest caveat: it is very generic and cannot be ranked for alone, so it works only if the method
   underneath has its own distinctive name.
   *Tagline: "From your first prompt to your first agent team."*
