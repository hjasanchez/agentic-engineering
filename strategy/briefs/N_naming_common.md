# Common rules for the three naming agents (read with 00_SHARED_CONTEXT.md)

## Hard constraints from Henrique (every candidate must satisfy all)
1. Contains a direct, unmistakable reference to AI, automation, LLMs, agents or machine intelligence (the word "AI",
   "Agent"/"Agentic", "LLM", "Automation"/"Autonomous", "Model", "Machine", "Bot", "Copilot"-class words, "Intelligence").
2. Short: ideally one or two words, at most three; at most ~14 characters for a one-word name; easy to say in a sales call
   and to put on a slide, an invoice and a LinkedIn headline.
3. Enterprise-credible: a COO or HR director would not be embarrassed to put it in a procurement form. No puns that need
   explaining, no cute misspellings unless they are instantly readable.
4. Can cover the whole offer: a curriculum (AI @ Workplace 101–601), a method (stances × stack, the diagnostic), a consulting
   practice (assessment, workshops), and later skills/software.
5. Implies, or at least permits, the core idea: the human stays in charge, knows which mode they are in, directs the machine.
   It must not sound like an AI product that does the work for you.

## What to avoid
- Names owned by incumbents: AI Fluency (Anthropic), Agent Boss / Frontier Firm (Microsoft), Co-Intelligence (Mollick),
  Intent Engineering (Nate B. Jones), Agentic Engineering (Karpathy, generic), Operator (OpenAI), Copilot (Microsoft),
  AgentOps (existing company), AIES (a conference), Directing Intelligence (a 2027 book), Articulate, Conductor, Cockpit,
  Agentcraft, Tokencraft, Workcraft, Specwork (all taken per research/R3_naming_and_brand_check.md; read its table).
- Generic descriptors that thousands use ("AI Training", "AI Consulting", "AI Academy", "AI Literacy").

## Output format (write to the path your brief names)
A Markdown table with 40 candidates: name | word roots and why | what it says to a non-technical office worker (one line) |
what it says to a CTO (one line) | risk you can foresee (collision, genericness, pronunciation) | your score 1–5.
Then: your top 8 with a two-sentence pitch each and a suggested tagline. Then: 5 "wildcards" that break one rule on purpose,
labelled with the rule they break, in case Henrique's constraints are too tight.
No web research in this pass (a separate agent checks collisions); generate from knowledge. Do not rank across agents.
Return ≤10 lines: your top 8 names, one line each.
