# Which mode are you in? A self-check

This is not a test. There are no right answers, nobody marks it, and you keep it. Answer for how you actually use AI for
schoolwork, not how you think you should. If you have never used it, answer for how you think you would.

**1. When you open the AI for schoolwork, what are you hoping comes back?** Circle one.

- **A** The answer, or the finished piece.
- **B** What has been written about the topic, gathered in one place.
- **C** Help working it out myself.
- **D** My own messy ideas, put in order.
- **E** Someone to pick holes in what I have done.

**2. Think of the last time you used AI for homework. What did you give it?**

- **A** The question or task, as it was set.
- **B** A topic, and a request for sources or facts on it.
- **C** The bit I was stuck on, and a request for a hint, not the answer.
- **D** My notes or my draft, and a request to organise them.
- **E** My draft or my argument, and a request to find the weak points.

**3. What did you do with what came back?**

- **A** Used it more or less as it was.
- **B** Took the facts or sources it gave me and used them.
- **C** Did the next step myself.
- **D** Rewrote my work in my own words, using the order it showed me.
- **E** Fixed my work where the criticism was fair.

**4. Which sentence sounds most like you?**

- **A** "It's quicker to just get the answer."
- **B** "I use it like a search engine that writes a summary."
- **C** "I'd rather it quizzed me than told me."
- **D** "It's good at making my ideas make sense."
- **E** "I use it to find what my teacher will criticise, before they do."

**5. If the AI disappeared tomorrow, what would you miss most?**

- **A** Getting answers fast.
- **B** Finding and summarising information.
- **C** Explanations when I am stuck.
- **D** Getting my thoughts into order.
- **E** Honest criticism of my work.

**6. Before work with AI help leaves your hands, how do you usually check it?** This one is not scored. Circle one.

- **a** I don't, usually.
- **b** I ask the same chat whether it is right.
- **c** I check it against my notes, the textbook or the original source.
- **d** I do one part myself and compare.
- **e** I ask a person who knows the topic.

## Your result

Count your letters for questions 1 to 5: A <span class="blank tiny"></span> B <span class="blank tiny"></span>
C <span class="blank tiny"></span> D <span class="blank tiny"></span> E <span class="blank tiny"></span>
The letter you circled most is the stance you usually use. If two tie, read both.

| | Stance | You expect | How it fails | The one rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Oracle**, the answer machine | Truth to a hard question (or, its twin the Genie, the finished piece) | Confident plausible prose taken as fact | Ask for the uncertainty before the answer. Treat the answer as a claim, not a verdict. Then try the Tutor. |
| **B** | **Librarian** | Synthesis of what is known | Confident retrieval of what was never said | Check the citation exists and says what is claimed. |
| **C** | **Tutor** | A path to the answer | Being taught toward a goal you never stated | State what you want to be able to do afterwards before the first question. |
| **D** | **Mirror** | My own thinking, clarified | Flattery mistaken for reflection | Ask for the structure of your argument back, not for agreement. |
| **E** | **Adversary** | Attack on my plan | Critique of the wrong thing | Name what you are defending and what would change your mind. |

**Mostly A?** That is where most people start, and it is the stance that can do the learning for you. Next time, try the Tutor:
"Don't tell me the answer yet. What questions should I be asking?"

**Question 6, who checks?** If you circled **a** or **b**, nobody outside the chat is checking. The checker is never the chat that
wrote it. Pick one of **c**, **d** or **e** for your next assignment.
