## The Oracle

<p class="tag">the answer machine</p>

**You expect** truth to a hard question.

**How it fails** Confident plausible prose taken as fact. You asked for truth and received plausible prose.

**The one rule** Ask for the uncertainty before the answer. Treat the answer as a claim, not a verdict.

**At school** *(made-up example)* Revising for history, you ask for the three causes of a war and the date it ended. The date is a
year out. You ask "which parts are you least sure of?" and check the date in the textbook.

---

## The Tutor

<p class="tag">a path to the answer</p>

**You expect** a path to the answer, not the answer.

**How it fails** Being taught toward a goal you never stated.

**The one rule** State what you want to be able to do afterwards before the first question.

**At school** *(made-up example)* "I have a test on Friday on quadratic equations, without a calculator. I want to solve them on my
own. Don't tell me the answer yet. What questions should I be asking?"

---

## The Mirror

<p class="tag">my own thinking, clarified</p>

**You expect** your own thoughts back, with more clarity and structure. The value is recognition, not novelty.

**How it fails** Flattery mistaken for reflection.

**The one rule** Ask for the structure of your argument back, not for agreement.

**At school** *(made-up example)* You paste your notes for an English essay: "List the claims I make, in order, and the evidence I
give for each. Don't tell me whether they are good." Two claims turn out to have no evidence.

---

## The Adversary

<p class="tag">attack my draft</p>

**You expect** an attack on your plan or draft: the holes found, the other side made strong.

**How it fails** Critique of the wrong thing.

**The one rule** Name what you are defending and what would change your mind.

**At school** *(made-up example)* Before a debate: "I'm arguing the town should build the bypass, because of air quality on the
high street. I'd change my mind if the traffic just moved to the villages. Attack that reason."

---

## The Librarian

<p class="tag">what has been said</p>

**You expect** a synthesis of what is known: what has been said, not what is.

**How it fails** Confident retrieval of what was never said.

**The one rule** Check the citation exists and says what is claimed.

**At school** *(made-up example)* For a geography project you ask for five sources on coastal erosion, with author, year and title.
You look each one up. One does not exist; another is about a different coast.

---

## Which mode are you in?

<p class="tag">how to use these cards</p>

**Before you type,** pick the card for the help you want, and say it out loud or write it in your mode log.

**Then do the one rule** on the card. It is the check that stance needs.

**Who checks?** Every piece of work with AI help has a checker, named before you start. The checker is never the chat that wrote it.

**Mostly an Oracle?** Most people are. Try the Tutor next time.
