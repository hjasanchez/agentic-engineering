The Fourteen Stances

Archetypes of human-AI interaction. Each stance is the contract you think you have with the AI before you type a word.

Most people hold one or two stances without naming them, usually the Oracle (truth to a hard question) and the Genie (grant my wish). Both are fragile, because they demand an authority the technology cannot reliably provide. The other twelve are quieter, and most of them are more useful. Each card gives the expectation, why the stance stays hidden, the boundary test where the source has one, the failure mode, and the cardinal rule.

Each card also runs its stance as a small field of tokens: what the contract does when it runs, and where it quietly fails. Only the cards on screen animate. Pointer or touch: you are the warm point.

Text from Archetypes of LLM-Human Interaction v3 by Henrique Jr. Sanchez. Where v3 states a cardinal rule explicitly (Interpreter, Executor, Miner) it is quoted; for the other stances the rule is the guardrail column of the Matrix. Load-bearing layers are from the Matrix.

Oracle

01 / 14

Layer: JudgmentConsumption

field every token converges on one bright point

Expectation
Coming to the LLM with the expectation of receiving truth in the form of an answer to a difficult question not easily knowable by the human.
Why it dominates
It is a zero-sum archetype: the human asks, the LLM delivers, and the human's cognitive load is minimized. That makes it seductive, and it demands an authority (truth without method) the technology cannot reliably provide.
Failure mode
It collapses when the LLM hallucinates or confabulates, because the user has outsourced their epistemic filter. They asked for truth and received plausible prose.

cardinal rule (from the matrix guardrail)

Ask for the uncertainty before the answer. Treat the answer as a claim, not a verdict.

Adopt the Tutor over the Oracle: “Don't tell me the answer yet. What questions should I be asking?”

Genie

02 / 14

Layer: PromptConsumption

field tokens fill exactly the shape that was asked for, and nothing else

Expectation
Coming to the LLM with the expectation of asking for a wish: a request with a discrete deliverable outcome which can be verified, and receiving it.
Why it dominates
Like the Oracle, it is a zero-sum archetype: the human asks, the LLM delivers, and the human's cognitive load is minimized. It demands deliverables without negotiation.
Failure mode
It collapses when the deliverable requires context the user did not provide, because wishes, by mythological contract, are literal and brittle.

cardinal rule (from the matrix guardrail)

Specify scope, boundaries and success criteria before you ask (an Intent Doc).

Adopt the Collaborator over the Genie: treat the first output as a rough joint draft. “Here is my intent and my constraints. Let's iterate.”

Tutor (or Socratic Midwife)

03 / 14

Layer: IntentConstruction

field a path forms; it leads wherever the tutor thinks you are going

Expectation
Not an answer, but a path to an answer. The human arrives confused and expects the LLM to diagnose gaps in understanding, ask clarifying questions, and guide discovery.
Why it stays hidden
It requires patience. The user must tolerate being questioned in return, which feels slower than the Oracle.
Failure mode
Being taught toward a goal you never stated.

cardinal rule (from the matrix guardrail)

State what you want to be able to do afterwards before the first question.

Robust against hallucination because it keeps the human in the reasoning loop.

Mirror

04 / 14

Layer: CoherenceConstruction

field the field returns your own position, reflected across the line

Expectation
The LLM reflects the user's own thoughts back with greater clarity, precision, or structure. The value is not in novelty but in recognition.
Why it stays hidden
It feels almost embarrassing to admit you are talking to a machine just to hear your own thoughts organized. Yet this is an enormous use case.
Failure mode
Flattery mistaken for reflection.

cardinal rule (from the matrix guardrail)

Ask for the structure of your argument back, not for agreement.

Adversary (or Devil's Advocate)

05 / 14

Layer: IntentNot placed in v3 table

field critique streams in; until you name what you defend, it lands on a decoy

Expectation
The human brings a belief, plan, or draft and expects the LLM to attack it, find holes, or steel-man the opposition.
Why it stays hidden
It requires the user to suspend ego and invite criticism from a non-human, which feels unnatural unless explicitly framed.
Failure mode
Critique of the wrong thing.

cardinal rule (from the matrix guardrail)

Name what you are defending and what would change your mind.

Librarian (not the Oracle)

06 / 14

Layer: EvaluationConsumption

field tokens leave the shelves for one synthesis; a few come from shelves that do not exist

Expectation
Retrieval and synthesis of known information, not prophecy. The user wants to know what has been said, not what is.
Why it stays hidden
It blurs with the Oracle, but the emotional contract is different. The Librarian admits fallibility; the Oracle does not.
Failure mode
Confident retrieval of what was never said.

cardinal rule (from the matrix guardrail)

Check the citation exists and says what is claimed.

Interpreter

07 / 14

Layer: EvaluationHinge: passive by default, active with the evidence trail

field material in, conclusions out; only the evidence trail shows which conclusions have a source

Expectation
The human brings the material (a paper, a contract, a dataset, a statute, a medical report) and expects the LLM to decode it: compress the complexity, say what it actually means, and draw out what conclusions or actions follow from it. “I don't have time to master this domain. Tell me what this thing means and what I should take from it.”
Why it stays hidden
It masquerades as its neighbors. It feels like the Librarian (the Librarian finds material; the Interpreter decodes material you already have) and like the Tutor (the Tutor builds understanding you keep; the Interpreter delivers conclusions you use). “Just reading, but outsourced” doesn't feel like a skill.
Boundary test
Did you hand the AI something to decode? If the AI has to go find the material, that's the Librarian. If the goal is lasting understanding rather than a conclusion, that's the Tutor. The Interpreter is the middle act: material in, meaning and conclusions out.
Failure mode
The most hallucination-prone archetype in the pantheon, because confabulated interpretation looks exactly like correct interpretation. An Oracle that errs is obviously wrong; an Interpreter that errs sounds insightful. And the user who needs an Interpreter is the least equipped person in the loop to catch the error.

cardinal rule

Never accept an interpretation you cannot trace back to the source. If you can't point to the sentence, clause, or row that supports the conclusion, you don't have an interpretation; you have a rumor in polished prose.

Simulator

08 / 14

Layer: ContextNot placed in v3 table

field two parties rehearse; without your real counterpart it is a generic scene

Expectation
The LLM becomes a sandbox. Simulate a negotiation, a historical figure, a market, a difficult conversation. The user wants to rehearse reality.
Why it stays hidden
It feels like “playing,” not working, though it is often the highest-value interaction.
Failure mode
A rehearsal of a scenario that is not yours.

cardinal rule (from the matrix guardrail)

Load the real constraints, the real counterpart, the real stakes.

“Simulate how a skeptical board member would respond to this proposal.”

Scribe (or Intern)

09 / 14

Layer: PromptNot placed in v3 table

field rough material becomes clean lines; the gaps are filled with marks you never wrote

Expectation
Transform rough material into polished form. Transcribe, format, expand bullet points, clean up code. The human provides the substance; the LLM provides the craft.
Why it stays hidden
It is humble. There is no magic in it, so users rarely name it as an archetype, yet it dominates actual usage.
Failure mode
Polished form over a hollow draft.

cardinal rule (from the matrix guardrail)

Provide the substance and the format; review for what was invented to fill gaps.

Executor

10 / 14

Layer: Harness + JudgmentConsumption: the passive extreme (staged oversight is its active form)

field a stream leaves on its own; the checkpoint line catches it

Expectation
Full delegation of an outcome. The human supplies the goal; the agent supplies the steps, the labor, and the intermediate decisions; the work happens where the human is not looking; the human reviews when it is done. A job, supervised after the fact.
Why it stays hidden
It is new (it barely existed before agents) and it doesn't register as a stance toward AI at all, because it feels like simply managing. It also hides inside the Genie's vocabulary (“I asked it to do X”).
Boundary test
Did you inspect the deliverable as the whole of the work (Genie), or is the deliverable just the end of a chain you never saw (Executor)? And did you supply the substance the craft works on (Scribe), or only the goal? Execution judgment is transferred; goal judgment is supposed to stay with you.
Failure mode
Automation complacency, and it fails on the human's side of the contract. Oversight atrophies because not-watching is the entire point of delegation; the agent completes the letter of the task and misses its point; many small, locally correct steps compound into one wrong outcome; and the failure surfaces later than in any other archetype.

cardinal rule

Delegation transfers labor, never responsibility. If you can't see where the work went, you don't have completed work; you have confident claims about completed work.

Guardrail. The guardrail is structural, not interpretive: plan before action, checkpoints at intermediate states, an explicit deviation report (“tell me everywhere you departed from the letter of the task, and why”), and nothing consequential ships uninspected.

Muse

11 / 14

Layer: (none)Consumption

field no target, no checkpoint: provocation only

Expectation
Not correctness, but provocation. The user wants lateral jumps, absurd juxtapositions, metaphors from unrelated domains.
Why it stays hidden
It contradicts the utility-focused narrative around AI. You cannot metric a muse.
Failure mode
There is no failure, only dross.

cardinal rule (from the matrix guardrail)

None. This is why the Muse stays hidden: it cannot be metric'd.

Miner

12 / 14

Layer: Context (writing)Construction

field tokens scatter; bright ore surfaces; pass over it to record it before the vein shifts

Expectation
Not an output but a space. The human treats the model as a vast, mostly unvisited space of possible outputs and works it like a vein: many variations generated cheaply, collected without immediate judgment, then selected and assayed later with cold attention.
Why it stays hidden
It hides inside the Muse (“I asked for ideas”). It also looks wasteful: generating seven outputs to keep one violates every efficiency instinct the interface trains. And most of what comes up is dross, so without selection discipline the dross rate reads as the tool failing.
Boundary test
Did you ask for one good thing, or for the shape of the space? The Muse receives a provocation and judges it on arrival. The Miner generates in volume, withholds judgment, selects late. If you are evaluating while you read, you are not mining; you are browsing.
Failure mode
Two errors in the same breath. Assaying nothing: the anomaly gets believed because it was wanted (sycophancy through the side door). Losing the vein: the ore is generated by sampling, not stored in the weights, so you cannot re-find a nugget by re-running the same prompt.

cardinal rule

If you didn't record it when you found it, you never had it.

Guardrail. The mined idea is ore, never a citation; nothing mined enters anything you stake something on until it has survived an independent check.

“Give me seven approaches from seven different angles. Do not evaluate them; I will choose.”

Therapist (or Confessor)

13 / 14

Layer: JudgmentConsumption

field a slow, breathing space; one ring stays clear: what you will not delegate

Expectation
A non-judgmental space to externalize thoughts. The LLM has no social stake in the user's life, making it a safe sounding board for half-formed ideas or emotional processing.
Why it stays hidden
The stigma around “talking to a machine” about human matters, plus legitimate concerns about emotional dependency.
Failure mode
Dependency; outsourcing judgment about your own life.

cardinal rule (from the matrix guardrail)

Decide in advance what you will not delegate.

Translator (between worlds)

14 / 14

Layer: ContextConstruction

field tokens cross a bridge; without the second map they land on a caricature

Expectation
Translate not just languages, but cultures and domains. Explain the engineer to the marketer, the doctor to the patient, the parent to the teenager.
Why it stays hidden
It requires the user to already hold two incomplete maps and know they need a bridge.
Failure mode
A bridge from one world to a caricature of the other.

cardinal rule (from the matrix guardrail)

Supply both maps; name both audiences.

No stance matches both filters. Try “All” on one of them.

§2 meta_pattern

Consumption vs. construction

There may be a deeper axis under the pantheon. The passive stances position the human as a recipient. The active stances position the human as a participant in the thinking. As AI becomes more embedded in serious work, the shift from passive to active may be the single most important cognitive upgrade users can make.

The meta-pattern, from Archetypes v3
Consumption (passive)Construction (active)
OracleTutor
GenieCollaborator
LibrarianTranslator
TherapistMirror
Interpreter (unverified)Interpreter (evidence-trail mode)
Muse (receives the surprise)Miner (works the vein)
Executor (blind delegation)Executor (staged oversight)

The Interpreter sits at the hinge: passive by default, active only when you demand the evidence trail. The Executor sits at the passive extreme, which is why its guardrail must be built out of structure; the Miner sits at the active extreme, which is why its guardrail is the journal. The Collaborator appears in this table as the active form of the Genie but is not one of the fourteen. The Adversary, the Simulator and the Scribe are not placed in the v3 table, so the filter shows them as “Not placed”.

Every stance is one habit away from its active form.
