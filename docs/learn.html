Curriculum

AI @ Workplace, 101 to 601

How to use the calculator, for people who can already do the math by hand.

Four levels are out, each a recorded talk with slides. Start where you are, not at the top. 101 to 301 are about chatbots: in a chatbot, you are between every turn. From 401 on, you set the goal and the guardrails, and then you are on the loop, not in it.

The levels

101

AI 101 @ Workplace

For absolute AI/prompt beginners. Video, 20:28.

What LLMs are, how to start prompting, what to watch out for: hallucinations, false confidence, slop, over-reliance.

“Intelligence is now cheap, but judgment is rare.”

  • Watch on YouTube
  • Slides (PDF on GitHub)

201

AI 201 @ Workplace

For regular chatbot users. Video, 49:40.

How LLMs “think”, the 6-element prompt framework, self-correction and meta-prompting, workflow design, task decomposition, quality control.

“Your LLM is only as good as your ability to direct it.”

  • Watch on YouTube
  • Slides (PDF on GitHub)
  • Prompts, workflows, templates (Google Drive)

301

AI 301 @ Workplace

For advanced chatbot users. Video, 1:02:35.

Custom bots and knowledge bases, context management, from chatbot to agent, the information map, failure patterns, the eval mindset.

“Your output is only as trustworthy as your ability to check it.”

  • Watch on YouTube
  • Slides (PDF on GitHub)

401

AI 401 @ Workplace

For agent users. Video, 1:25:14.

Upgrading to agents, harness engineering, determinism in agentic workflows, add-ons (skills, MCP), introducing loops, the translation barrier.

“Your agent is only as reliable as the harness you build around it.”

  • Watch on YouTube
  • Slides (PDF on GitHub)

501

AI 501 @ Workplace Coming

Local agents, and going “hands-free”: letting it run without you reading every output.

601

AI 601 @ Workplace Coming

Dark factories. A full description of the scope is still to be written.

Not sure where to start?

Take the diagnostic: it recommends a level from the stance you use most and how much of the work you watch. Before any of it, the 101 advice holds: check which tools your IT department has vetted, and lean on your own experience in your field to know what to do, how to do it, and when a task is finished.
